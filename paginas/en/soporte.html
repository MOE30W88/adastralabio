02 — Support

Privacy policy

Last updated: October 17, 2026

AD ASTRA is an astronomical clock that runs in your browser. The calculations are done on your device: the site has no user accounts, forms or database of its own, and it does not collect personal data to build profiles, track you or advertise. Some functions send information to third-party services, as explained in this policy. This policy explains what data is used, which external services it may be shared with, and what you can decide.

1. Controller and contact

The person responsible for the site is Moisés Soriano, author of AD ASTRA. For any question about this policy, write to the contact email shown in the footer of the home page.

2. Summary

AD ASTRA does not use cookies, analytics, advertising or cross-site tracking; it does not ask for your name, email or any personal data; and it does not sell or share data. Besides the normal connection to the site's hosting (section 3), the only thing that leaves your browser toward a third party is the text or coordinates queried in the place-name service (section 3), and only if you grant the location permission or use the search box or the map.

3. What data is processed and who receives it

  • Your location. It is used to calculate the Sun, the Moon, the horizon and the eclipses visible from your place. Your browser obtains it only if you grant permission, or you provide it by typing a place or choosing it on the map. The coordinates are used on your device, are not sent to any AD ASTRA server and are not stored: they live only while the page is open.
  • Place name (Nominatim, from OpenStreetMap). To show the city and country, and to search for places by name, your browser queries the Nominatim service (nominatim.openstreetmap.org), operated by the OpenStreetMap Foundation. It sends the coordinates (when you grant your location or choose a point on the map) or the text you type in the search box, together with the site's language (es or en) so that it replies in that language. That service also receives your IP address and the usual browser data, and processes them according to its privacy policy and its usage policy. These queries are made only if you grant the location permission, search for a place or choose a point on the map. Your browser asks for the location permission when you open the site; if you have already granted it, the place-name query is repeated every time you open it. The responses are not stored.
  • Map. The selector map is drawn with data included in the site itself (Natural Earth). No maps or images are loaded from external servers.
  • Typography. The site's typeface (Playfair Display) is served from the site itself. Google and other font services are not contacted.
  • Preferences in your browser. The site stores two preferences in your browser's local storage: the color mode (tema-preferencia) and the language (idioma). The language is saved from the first visit, according to your browser's language, and is updated if you change it with the ES/EN button. There is no other storage: your location is not saved. They are not cookies, they do not identify anyone, they are not sent to any server and you can delete them from your browser settings.
  • Hosting. The site is published on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.). Like any web server, the hosting provider may process certain technical data of each connection (such as your IP address), in accordance with its privacy statement and terms. The author of AD ASTRA does not receive or manage those infrastructure records.

4. What the site does not do

It does not create profiles, does not make automated decisions about you, does not use first-party or third-party cookies, does not include analytics or advertising tools, does not embed social networks and does not ask you for data in order to use it.

5. What the data is used for and on what basis

Only to display the clock: calculating the astronomical positions of your place, showing local time, naming your locality and remembering your preferences. The location and place queries are used because you request or authorize them; the preferences, so that the site works the way you left it.

6. Retention

AD ASTRA does not retain any data on its own servers. The coordinates live while the page is open. The preferences remain in your browser until you delete them. The third parties mentioned retain the data they receive according to their own policies.

7. Third-party services, links and transfers

Nominatim/OpenStreetMap and GitHub are third-party services with their own terms, and their servers may be in a country other than yours. The contact email and the GitHub link in the footer open external applications or sites, which process your data according to their policies.

8. Your rights

Toward AD ASTRA: the site does not retain personal data of its own, so there is no data of yours to consult, correct or delete on it (which does not mean that no third-party service processes data related to your visit, as explained in sections 3 and 7). You can deny or withdraw the location permission from your browser settings, delete the stored preferences and stop using the place search whenever you wish. To exercise your rights over the data received by third-party services, contact each one: the OpenStreetMap Foundation (Nominatim) for place queries, and GitHub, Inc. for the hosting. If you live in a place with a data protection law, you have the right to access, rectify, erase, object to and restrict the processing of your data, and to lodge a complaint with the data protection authority of your country. You can also write to the contact indicated above.

9. Minors

The site does not deliberately request personal data from minors nor require registration to use it. If you are a minor, we recommend using the site with the supervision of an adult, especially if you are going to share your location.

10. Security

The site is served over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) and does not maintain a user database, so there is no database of its own to protect. No system is infallible; if you detect a security problem, let us know through the contact indicated.

11. Changes

If the site changes the way it processes data, this policy will be updated and the date above will be revised.

04 — Support

Frequently asked questions

Is it a real-time clock?

Yes. It shows the time at your place and the astronomical positions of that instant. You can also change the date and time, or speed up time with the side controls, to see how the sky changes.

Why does the dial have 24 hours?

Because the Sun goes around once a day. With 24 divisions, noon is at the top, midnight at the bottom and the hour hand makes a single turn per day. More in Infographics.

Why does it ask for my location?

To calculate the altitude of the Sun and the Moon, the horizon, sunrise and sunset times and the eclipses visible from your place. If you do not share it, you can type a location by hand. Read the privacy policy.

Why do the Sun and the Moon look dim in the lower half?

Because in that half of the dial it is night at your place: they are below the horizon. They are drawn dimmed so that they do not disappear.

How precise are the calculations?

It depends on the phenomenon: the lunar phase and eclipses use high-precision models; the Sun and the planets use simple approximations. Everything is measured and explained in Scope.

Are the zodiac signs astrology?

No. The clock shows the ecliptic longitude of the Sun, divided into 12 sectors of 30°. It makes no predictions or horoscopes, and those sectors do not match the boundaries of the real constellations.

Why does the Chinese year start in February?

Because the Chinese calendar is lunisolar: the year begins with the Lunar New Year, which falls between January and February. The clock calculates it from the new moons and the solstices.

Why are the planets on the clock not to scale?

Because the real distances would not fit on the screen: Neptune is about 30 times the Earth's distance. The clock compresses large distances with a power scale. It is for ordering and comparing, not for measuring.

Does it work offline?

Once the page has loaded, the calculations are done in your browser. Offline, the name of your city and the results of the place search do not appear, because they depend on an external service.

Can I use it to decide when to observe an eclipse?

You can use it as a guide to when and from where it occurs, but confirm with official sources and never look at the Sun without certified protection.

I found an error. How do I report it?

Write to the email in the footer of the home page with the date, time and location you were using and a screenshot if you can.
