Astronomical clock

The sky, time and the Sun's position in a single sphere.

The AD ASTRA clock condenses a lot of information into a single drawing. Here each piece is explained with an image, a few key figures and three steps to read it without prior knowledge. The formulas are in Mathematics and the precision of each calculation is in Scope.

Clock

Time turned into hands, marks and a figure eight that the Sun draws over the year.

Clock

Clock background

The whole clock is drawn around a single point, its center. Every mark, hand or planet is located by saying how far away it is and in which direction.

600; 600center of the clock
0°top, 12 h
clockwisedirection in which the angle grows

How to read it on the clock

  1. Start at the center of the clock.
  2. Turn from the top, like a hand, toward the direction of the object.
  3. Move along that direction the indicated distance: there it is.

Keep in mind. The angle is counted as on a clock (from the top and to the right), not as on a geometry protractor.

Clock

Frame and hour scale

The dial has 24 hours, not 12: the hand makes a single turn per day. Noon is at the top and midnight at the bottom.

24dial divisions
15°per hour
12 htop; 0 h bottom

How to read it on the clock

  1. Find noon: right at the top.
  2. Count 15° for each elapsed hour, to the right.
  3. At 18 h the hour falls on the right; at 6 h, on the left.

Keep in mind. The Roman numerals on the frame repeat from I to XII twice, once for each half of the day.

Clock

Minute scale

A scale of 60 marks runs around the dial: each one is a minute. A full turn equals one hour.

60marks, one per minute
6°per minute
5minutes between long marks

How to read it on the clock

  1. Find the long mark closest to the minute hand.
  2. Count by fives up to it.
  3. Add the short marks left over.

Keep in mind. The top mark is labeled 60 instead of 0, as on traditional clocks.

Clock

Hour hand

The short hand marks the time at the chosen place and makes one turn per day. It changes color when the Sun is below the horizon.

1turn per day
15°per hour
0.25°per minute

How to read it on the clock

  1. Look at where the hand points.
  2. Compare with the 24 h scale: top is noon, bottom is midnight.
  3. Its color tells you whether it is day or night.

Keep in mind. An ordinary 12 h clock turns twice as fast; this one makes half a turn every 12 hours.

Clock

Minute hand

The long hand runs along the minute scale and completes one turn every hour. It moves continuously, not in jumps.

24turns per day
6°per minute
1 : 24ratio to the hour hand

How to read it on the clock

  1. Follow the long hand out to the outer scale.
  2. Read the minute at the nearest mark.
  3. Combine it with the hour from the short hand.

Keep in mind. While the hour hand makes one turn, the minute hand makes 24: on this dial the ratio is double that of a 12 h clock.

Clock

Second hand

The thinnest hand makes one turn every minute. It follows the same time as the others: if you speed up or stop the clock, the second hand goes along.

60 sper turn
6°per second
smoothcontinuous motion

How to read it on the clock

  1. Find the thinnest hand.
  2. Read its position on the minute scale.
  3. If you change the speed of time, watch it turn faster or slower.

Keep in mind. At real speed it looks almost still; it is an easy way to check that the clock is alive.

Clock

Analemma

If you photograph the Sun at the same clock time for a year, it is not always in the same place: it draws an elongated figure eight called the analemma.

±23.44°vertical range (declination)
≈ ±16 minhorizontal range (equation of time)
1 yearto complete the eight

How to read it on the clock

  1. The height of the eight tells how high the Sun is according to the season.
  2. The width tells how far ahead or behind the Sun is compared with the clock.
  3. The clock shows solar time and civil time on the sides.

Keep in mind. The difference reaches about 16 minutes in early November and about 14 in mid-February, in the opposite direction.

The Sun is drawn on the dial at the clock's time, with a brightness that depends on its altitude above the place's horizon.

invisiblebelow the horizon

How to read it on the clock

  1. Locate the Sun on the hour scale.
  2. Look at its brightness: it tells how high it is.
  3. Compare it with the layer's horizon.

Keep in mind. The Sun calculation is a simple approximation, good for drawing, not for precision ephemerides.

Day and night

Moon

The Moon shares the dial with the Sun. How far it is from the Sun indicates the phase: next to it, new moon; on the opposite side, full moon.

0°new moon, with the Sun
90°quarters
180°full moon, opposite

How to read it on the clock

  1. Find the Sun and the Moon on the dial.
  2. Measure how far apart they are.
  3. The separation tells you the approximate phase.

Keep in mind. By day the Moon does not disappear: it stays at 15 % light, very faint.

Day and night

Planets above the horizon

Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn may appear on the edge of the ring with their name, but only those that can be seen with the naked eye at that moment are drawn.

3°minimum altitude above the horizon
8°minimum separation from the Sun
5possible planets, from Mercury to Saturn

How to read it on the clock

  1. Find the named dots on the edge of the ring.
  2. Each dot is a planet you can see right now; those below the horizon or lost in the Sun's glare are not drawn.
  3. The larger and brighter the dot, the easier to see (Venus and Jupiter stand out).

Keep in mind. The position on the dial indicates where the planet is relative to the Sun, not how high it appears: a planet high up before dawn appears in the upper half, like the full Moon at midnight.

Day and night

Horizon

The horizon is the line that separates the visible sky from the part beneath your feet. The observer stands on it, at the center.

−0.833°sunrise and sunset threshold
34′lift due to the atmosphere
16′apparent radius of the Sun

How to read it on the clock

  1. The Sun rises when its upper edge peeks out.
  2. That happens with the center slightly below the horizon.
  3. The length of the day comes from sunrise and sunset.

Keep in mind. The atmosphere bends light: we see the Sun a little before it is, geometrically, above the horizon.

Zodiac

Zodiac band

The zodiac is the Sun's apparent path throughout the year, divided into 12 signs of 30°. On the clock it is a ring that turns around the Sun.

12signs of 30°

How to read it on the clock

Keep in mind. It is the tropical zodiac, counted from the equinox, not from the constellations.

Solar system

Earth's orbit

The Earth travels along a nearly circular ellipse with the Sun at one focus. Its position is calculated from six orbital numbers.

0.0167eccentricity: almost a circle
0.983 AUperihelion
1.017 AUaphelion

How to read it on the clock

  1. The blue dot is the Earth.
  2. Its angle on the dial is the Sun's longitude.
  3. It moves faster near perihelion (January).

Keep in mind. On the clock the orbit hardly looks flattened: it ranges from 174.1 to 175.9.

Rotation

Rotation axis

The Earth turns on an axis tilted 23.44°. The clock shows it with an arc and writes the speed of your surface as it turns.

23.44°tilt
465.1 m/sspeed at the equator
66.56°latitude of the polar circles

How to read it on the clock

  1. Observe the tilt of the axis relative to the vertical.
  2. Read the speed at the lower end.
  3. The higher the latitude, the lower the speed.

Keep in mind. The speed is that of the rotation only: it does not include the Earth's motion around the Sun (about 29.8 km/s).
